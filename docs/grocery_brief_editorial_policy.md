# Grocery Brief: authority and factual verification standard

Status: proposed editorial policy. Drafting, review and publishing must remain separate.

## Editorial promise
Every Grocery Brief story answers three questions: **What happened? What is actually verified? What should a grocery shopper do next?** An article must add distinct Youdle value beyond summarizing the source: an original decision checklist, shopping plan, clearly explained methodology, expert-verified comparison, or firsthand grocer/community information that Youdle genuinely has permission to report.

Do not present generic paraphrases as original reporting. Do not claim a Youdle survey, original product test, in-stock result, discount, coupon, health outcome, reader preference, or price comparison unless the underlying evidence exists and the methodology and date can be shown.

## Editorial ledger (private, not public article link-stuffing)
For each story, the reviewer needs a source list with exact URLs, publication dates, when the underlying event happened, publisher and reporting/agency distinction, and any material uncertainties. Specific claims require verified backing, including all names, product names, dates, numbers, lot codes, UPCs, locations, quoted people and defined study populations. Store official sources where available, especially FDA and USDA notices. An external source name can be credited in public prose; the URL can remain in the editorial record except safety-critical notices, for which readers should be able to reach the primary notice.

## Originality and authority check
The Youdle Take must provide an insight the source did not already hand the reader. If it offers a suggested recipe or meal plan that Youdle has not kitchen-tested, label it as a plan or template, not a tested result. If presenting a measured trend, include the sample, geography, timeframe and data owner; never extrapolate a platform-specific number to all Americans. Do not imply anecdotal community sightings represent verified inventory.

## Internal links
Use only verified, topic-specific Youdle feature routes and relevant previously published Youdle stories that have passed a content audit. Never invent URLs or insist on archive links when the older article is not credible. Do not use unrelated marketing CTAs. Validate destination routes at publish time. Keep publication-to-publication source links in the editor ledger unless the audience must access primary safety or other critical evidence.

## Hallucination and safety preflight
1. Re-read every material claim against the original primary evidence and, where useful, an independent secondary source.
2. Distinguish reported, planned, launched, national, select markets and verified in stock.
3. Identify the limits of estimates, surveys, sample sizes and company-supplied claims.
4. For recalls, check the FDA/USDA notice on publication day for updates; verify product, UPC, lot/best-by code, affected locations, medical hazard and official consumer action.
5. Review health and allergy copy with heightened scrutiny. Do not invent treatment advice or describe any food as safe for every allergy.
6. Verify all Youdle internal links. Keep external source URLs in editorial notes.
7. Require a named human editor to attest sources, claims and links **before** changing status to reviewed and publishing.

An approval checkbox is an accountability gate, not independent proof of truth. An external publisher's report is a source, not a permission to copy its prose or photos. Image rights and publication-specific disclaimers still need review.

## Publishing metrics (not vanity rankings)
Track returning visits, search-driven useful answers, article-to-feature click-through, corrections, source freshness, and high-value shopping actions. Review recurring accuracy corrections every week. Avoid extra search calls or automatic expensive LLM loops just to fill a weekly quota.

## Release rule
Article status should progress draft -> reviewed (after documented human checks) -> published. Never send a draft to Blogger as live directly. Editing approved copy should require another review before first publication. Existing published stories and known old source-claim problems need a separate audit; updating the prompt does not repair them.
